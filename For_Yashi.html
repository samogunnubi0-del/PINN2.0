    What getting a 1400 really means
    • You do not have to be perfect. A 1400 is around a 700 in each section, so you can miss some questions, especially the hard Module 2 ones, and still get there.
    • For math, use Desmos whenever it saves you time. Let Module 1 be where you collect the points you know, then do your best on the harder questions. Remember the regression and tilde (~) tricks, but do not force them into every problem.
    • For English, start with the questions you feel best about. If the long reading questions slow you down, save them for later. For transitions, first ask whether the sentence is adding something, showing a contrast, or giving a reason.
    • Do not leave anything blank. If time is running out, take your best guess and move on. One question does not decide the whole test.
    Desmos moves worth locking
    • Solve: graph y = left and y = right. Click the intersection. That x is the answer.
    • System: graph both. Intersection = (x, y).
    • Data: + Table, then y1 ~ mx1 + b or y1 ~ ax1^2 + bx1 + c.
    • Unknown constants: replace = with ~ (tilde, Shift+` ). Desmos fills a, b, k.
    • Practice on desmos.com/testing so the SAT keyboard matches.
