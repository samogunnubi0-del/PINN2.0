🩺 the do pathway

quick breakdown of osteopathic medicine and nearby schools.

what's the difference between a do and an md?

they're both fully licensed doctors. same surgeries, same prescriptions, same residencies (pediatrics, ob-gyn, cardiology, family med).

the main difference is do schools do about 200 extra hours of hands-on musculoskeletal training (omt) and focus more on listening to the whole patient instead of just rushing a diagnosis, which fits what you wrote in your essay.

pcom (philadelphia, pa)

about 2 hours from maryland

one of the main do schools in the northeast with big hospital networks in pa and de.

vcom (blacksburg, va)

mid-atlantic

focuses a lot on community health and underserved clinics where doctors take time with patients.

rowan-virtua som (new jersey)

regional public do school

solid public program in the region with high residency match rates.

At eleven years old I vowed to never set foot in a doctors office past my eighteenth birthday – a delusional idea – and eventually enter the legal system to fight back against my injustice.

One might ask why I had such a disdain towards the doctors office, but the truth is that I never had a logical reason. It might've been the sheer fact that one person could look at my body and conjure up a diagnosis without my say. Or the fact that I had to face my unhealthy practices embarrassed me to no end. My resentment continued until my sophomore year of high school, where I took my first course in African American Studies. In this class my eyes were opened to numerous wrongs that the Black community has endured in many systems, one being the health care system. I learned that black women – such as myself– had a long history of inequities when it came to their experiences in the medical field. We were statistically treated with less courtesy, respect, and empathy when compared to the average patient.
