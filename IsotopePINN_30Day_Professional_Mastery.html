Module 1 / From zero Day 1–2

An isotope is the same element with a different neutron count

Atom

Plain English: The smallest piece of an element that still behaves like that element. A tiny nucleus of protons and neutrons, with electrons around it.
In this project: We count nuclei, not molecules. N is “how many atoms of this isotope are in the target.”

Element

Plain English: Defined by proton number. 88 protons is always radium. 89 protons is always actinium.
In this project: (n,2n) stays on radium. Beta-minus decay changes radium into actinium because a neutron becomes a proton.

Isotope

Plain English: Same protons, different neutrons, so different mass number. Ra-226 and Ra-225 are both radium, but not interchangeable.
In this project: The five tracked isotopes are Ra-226, Ra-225, Ac-225, Ra-227, and Ac-227.

Actinium-22589225The desired medical isotope

Say it aloud: “Ra-226 and Ac-225 are different elements. Ra-226 and Ra-225 are the same element with one neutron difference. That one neutron is the whole production idea.”
Retrieval drill
Why does (n,2n) keep the element radium, while beta-minus changes it to actinium?
Reveal answer
(n,2n) changes mass number by removing one neutron, so Z stays 88. Beta-minus converts a neutron into a proton, so Z becomes 89 and the element becomes actinium, while A stays the same.

Module 1 / Clocks Day 2

Half-life is a clock, not a countdown to zero atoms

Radioactivity

Plain English: An unstable nucleus can change by itself, emitting a particle or energy. That random event is a decay.
In this project: Decay is one of two ways inventories change. The other is a neutron-induced reaction while the beam or reactor is on.

Half-life T1/2

Plain English: The time for half of a large sample to decay. After one half-life, half remains; after two, one quarter remains. It never “finishes” in finite time.
In this project: Half-lives set the five clocks. Ra-227 is 42.2 minutes. Ra-226 is about 1,600 years. That mismatch is stiffness.
λ = ln(2) / T1/2    then    A = λN

Decay constant λ

Probability per unit time that one nucleus decays. Units: 1/time, for example h-1.

Activity A

Decays per second, not “how many atoms.” One becquerel (Bq) is one decay per second. A curie is 3.7×1010 Bq.

Teacher trap

A timestamp mismatch can look like a model error. Always name the isotope and the exact clock time of an activity.

Produced vs recovered: atoms made in the target, activity at end of bombardment, activity after cooling, and activity after chemistry are four different numbers. Berkeley recovered only about 37–60% of produced Ac-225 in the tested separations.
Retrieval drill
Convert a half-life into λ, then into activity. What must stay consistent?
Reveal answer
λ = ln(2)/T½ with T½ in the same time unit you want for λ. Then A = λN. Convert Bq, hours, and atom counts so every unit cancels correctly. Name the reference time.

Practice

Ac-225 T½ = 9.920 d. Compute λ in h^-1. Then if N is 1e12 atoms, compute A in Bq.

Module 2 / Motivation Day 3

Radiation can damage DNA. The question is where, and how densely.

Your project does not treat patients. The medical story is why Ac-225 is valuable enough that production planning is a real scientific problem.

Cancer, simply

Cells that grow out of control. Metastatic disease has spread. Some tumors resist chemo and ordinary radiation because they can repair isolated DNA damage.

Beta radiation

Fast electrons. Relatively long range in tissue (millimeters). Lower linear energy transfer. Can cause single-strand DNA breaks that cells may repair, and can irradiate nearby healthy tissue.

Alpha radiation

A helium-4 nucleus (2 protons + 2 neutrons). Heavy, short range (tens of micrometers, a couple of cell diameters), very dense ionization. Tends to cause complex double-strand DNA breaks.

Say it aloud: “Alpha particles dump a lot of energy in a very short path. If you can park an alpha emitter on a cancer cell, you can damage that cell while nearby tissue is more likely to be spared. That is the idea of targeted alpha therapy.”
Do not say: “My model cures cancer” or “this will treat 50,000 patients.” Those are motivation numbers from literature demand estimates, not results you measured.
Retrieval drill
In one minute, explain why alphas are discussed for therapy and why that still does not make your model a medical device.
Reveal answer
Alphas have short range and high LET, so they can kill targeted cells. My work is a computational production-screening study. It never administered a dose, proved safety, or measured clinical outcome.

Cite, don’t invent

Kratochwil 2016 and Morgenstern reviews are the clinical context. You are not a physician.

Module 3 / Collision language Day 6

A reaction rate is “how hard the rain is” times “how big the umbrella is”

k(E) = φ(E) σ(E)

Flux φ

Plain English: How many neutrons pass through a square centimeter per second.
Units: n·cm-2·s-1. In the model this is a supplied operating condition, not something the network invents.

Cross section σ

Plain English: How likely that neutron is to cause that specific reaction at that energy. Not a literal geometric circle, but it has area units.
Units: barns. 1 b = 10-24 cm2. σ depends strongly on energy.

Rate k

Plain English: Probability per target atom per second that the reaction happens.
Units: s-1 or h-1. This is what V4 tries to predict after geometry/spectrum folding.
spectrum-averaged σ̄ = [∫ σ(E) φ(E) dE] / [∫ φ(E) dE]
Why a single σ number can lie: O’Connor 1960 measured σ(n,2n) = 1.60 ± 0.20 b at 14.5 MeV. A fast reactor spectrum has only a small fraction of neutrons that hard. Using 1.60 b as if it were a reactor-average value overpredicts Ac-225. That is the Joyo gap.
Retrieval drill
Show that φσ has units of 1/time, and explain why 14.5 MeV σ is not a Joyo yield.
Reveal answer
cm^-2 s^-1 × cm^2 = s^-1. A monoenergetic 14.5 MeV cross section does not equal a spectrum-averaged reactor rate. Most reactor neutrons sit below the 6.42 MeV threshold.

Fluence

Fluence is flux integrated over time: total neutrons per area. Berkeley needs fluence, not only a flux slogan.

Module 3 / Energy Day 6

The desired reaction is a door with a locked energy latch

Threshold

Plain English: Below a certain neutron energy, (n,2n) essentially does not happen because there is not enough energy to knock out two neutrons.
In this project: Evaluated tables start near 6.4218 MeV. That is why “fast” is not a vibe word. It is a cutoff.

Spectral bias, preview

Neural nets like smooth, low-frequency patterns. A sharp “off below 6.42, on above” is hard for them. That is why energy was passed through sine/cosine Fourier features (Tancik et al.).

Module 4 / The model Day 9

This is the entire reduced physics world

dN226/dt = −(λ226 + k2n + kγ) N226
dN225/dt = k2n N226 − λ225 N225
dNAc225/dt = λ225 N225 − λAc225 NAc225
dN227/dt = kγ N226 − λ227 N227
dNAc227/dt = λ227 N227 − λAc227 NAc227
Compact form: dN/dt = A N, where A is a 5×5 matrix of those coefficients. For constant rates this is a linear chain. That is why an exact matrix exponential exists, and why “ML vs a five-nuclide solver” is not the same contest as “ML vs OpenMC.”

Module 4 / Numerical physics Day 10

Stiffness is why V1 died. It is not a speed trophy.

Plain English

The system has a very fast process and a very slow process at the same time. If you take big time steps, the fast piece explodes numerically. If you take tiny steps, the slow piece takes forever to compute.

Module 5 / From zero Day 15

A model is a flexible function. Training picks its numbers.

Feature

An input. Time, flux, energy, distance, starting inventory.

Target / label

The answer you want, here a trajectory or two reaction rates. In this project, labels often come from Radau, not from a hospital.

Weights

The adjustable numbers inside the network. Not nuclear weights. Not statistical survey weights unless you say so.

Loss

A score of wrongness. Training tries to lower it. A low loss is not automatically physical truth.

Epoch

One pass through the training set. More epochs can overfit. Best epoch is a choice that must not peek at the final test.

Overfitting

Memorizing training cases so new cases get worse. Your checkpoint crisis was a cousin of this: optimistic validation.

One training step: predict → compare to labels and/or physics → compute gradients → update weights. That is not thinking. It is numerical optimization.
LSTM is not human memory. “Long Short-Term Memory” is a gated recurrent network that carries a numerical state along a sequence of time points.

Module 5 / Three tools Day 16

You used three model families on purpose

MLP (feed-forward)

Plain English: A stack of layers. Each time point is scored somewhat independently unless you add extra structure.
In this project: V1 vanilla MLP failed. V2 was an MLP-PINN. V3 compared against a parameter-matched MLP (2.91% vs V3 0.97% on the matched modeled endpoint test).

LSTM

Plain English: A sequence model with gates that keep or forget numerical state as time walks forward.
In this project: V3 PI-LSTM, 2 layers, hidden size 256, 64 time points, 1,400 Radau trajectories. V4 also used LSTMs, but sequence length was one, so memory was not tested there.

Gaussian process (GP)

Plain English: A probabilistic interpolator. Similar inputs give similar outputs, with a built-in uncertainty blob.
In this project: V4 baseline for small smooth tabular rate data. Raw ±2 SD joint coverage was only 2.1%, so calibration was mandatory.
Why a GP is a serious baseline: on small, smooth, static tables, a fancy network should not win by default. V4’s scientific winner was “none.”
Retrieval drill
Which version used an LSTM as a sequence model, and why can V4 not prove that memory helps?
Reveal answer
V3 is sequential over 64 times. V4 used one-step geometry/rate features, so recurrence had no real sequence to remember.

PINN is not a fourth architecture

Physics-informed is a training/structure idea. You can have a PINN-MLP or a PI-LSTM.

Module 5 / Architecture Day 18

Four tools, each with a human reason

1. Fourier features (Tancik)

Pass energy and time through sines and cosines at several frequencies. Helps the net see sharp thresholds and fast transients instead of only smooth curves. V3: 8 energy bands, later 16 time bands.

2. Hard initial condition (Lagaris)

N(t) = [1 − α(t)] N(0) + α(t) Nnet(t),   α(0) = 0

Time zero is locked to the input inventory. Stops “mass from nowhere” at t = 0. α uses a learnable per-species timescale.

3. Integrated trapezoidal loss (Nasiri & Dargazany)

rk = Nk+1 − Nk − (Δt/2)[f(Nk)+f(Nk+1)]

Punish interval imbalance instead of instantaneous dN/dt. Jaden Palmer pushed this after V2 overshoots. Limit: trapezoid is approximate on long log-spaced steps for 42.2-minute Ra-227.

4. Jacobian row-norm (Bento)

Ac-225 is tiny next to Ra-226. Without balancing, the loss ignores the medicine. Divide each residual by that row’s scale so trace species count.

Also in the code: softplus keeps inventories nonnegative; optional v2 distillation; overshoot penalty on Ra-227; later exact-exponential residual option that must not be claimed for a run that did not use it.
Retrieval drill
Give one sentence each for Fourier, hard IC, integrated loss, and Jacobian balancing.
Reveal answer
Fourier exposes scales; hard IC locks t=0; trapezoid enforces integrated Bateman balance; Jacobian stops bulk Ra-226 from drowning Ac-225 in the loss.

Abandoned: MC dropout

Jaden: randomly dropping neurons wrecks physical consistency. Replaced by conformal / later calibration ideas.

A useful surrogate must know when not to answer

Accuracy

How close is the point prediction? Median and p95 relative error.

Coverage

How often does the interval contain the true answer? V4 calibrated joint coverage: 95.8% (46/48) for both models.

OOD rejection

Out-of-distribution: an input unlike training. The guard refused 184/184 constructed outside-range cases and falsely rejected 0/48 locked in-domain families.

Coverage is not accuracy. A huge interval can cover everything and still be useless. Rejection is not “OOD accuracy.” You do not report accuracy on rejected cases.
GP lesson: raw ±2 SD joint coverage was 2.1% (1/48). Two standard deviations are near 95% only under assumptions. You tested the assumption and it failed until calibration.
Retrieval drill
How are calibration and OOD rejection different?
Reveal answer
Calibration resizes intervals for accepted cases. OOD rejection refuses to answer when the input is unlike the domain, rather than pretending the interval is still trustworthy.

p95

About 95% of errors are at or below this tail number. A pretty median can hide a disastrous tail that mis-ranks schedules.

Module 6 / Metric alignment Day 23

Struggle 4: the model looked better while Ac-225 at the end went to zero

relative endpoint error = |pred − truth| / |truth|  →  if pred ≈ 0, error ≈ 1 (100%)

Train ≠ score

Smooth-L1 over 64 log-spaced times barely punishes a tiny product at the last step. Causal physics weights even emphasize early times. Run A: trajectory medians improved while checkpoint “best” locked near 1.0.

Curriculum mismatch

Stiffness curriculum trained on destiffened ODEs (rate_scale 100 → 1) while “best” always scored full-stiffness Radau. Run A2: endpoint error exploded (~1e27) and best froze at 1.0 by epoch 25.

Lesson: curriculum is not always bad. It is bad when train stiffness ≠ score stiffness. A2b turned curriculum off and still had a brutal bare relative-endpoint loss. Metric design is research, not a footnote.
Story drill
Why does pred = 0 give ~100% relative error, and why did trajectory loss hide it?
Reveal answer
|0 − truth|/truth = 1. A mean over 64 steps lets 63 earlier points look fine while the harvest time is empty.
