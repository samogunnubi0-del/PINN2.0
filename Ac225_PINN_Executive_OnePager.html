Can AI help make a scarce cancer-therapy isotope faster to plan?

I’m building a physics-constrained neural network that predicts how Actinium-225 (Ac-225) — a rare isotope used in targeted cancer therapy — builds up when radium is irradiated. The goal: keep the physics honest, run thousands of “what-if” plans in milliseconds, and share the work so others can help improve it.

In plain English

The need

Ac-225 can destroy cancer cells with alpha particles, but the world makes very little of it. Planning production means lots of slow math.

What I built

An AI “shortcut”

A model that learns from a trusted physics simulator, then answers similar questions much faster — without inventing impossible mass.

Why it matters

Faster design sweeps

Engineers could scan many flux / time options quickly, then verify favorites with full solvers — not replace the lab.

The problem (a little more detail)

Making Ac-225 in a reactor from Ra-226 requires solving the Bateman equations — a stiff chain of five isotopes. Some species change in minutes; others over centuries (stiffness > 20,000,000:1). Classic solvers are accurate but slow for large design searches. Plain neural nets are fast but can break conservation laws (negative atoms, wrong mass).

Mentorship — Jaden Palmer, NCSU ARTISANS Lab

Technical review from Jaden Palmer (PhD researcher, NC State Nuclear Engineering) pushed the project toward integrated / exact physics losses, conformal uncertainty (instead of MC Dropout), and a fair PI-LSTM vs baseline comparison.

Technical approach — PI-LSTM (Results-6)

  • Physics-Informed LSTM with hard initial conditions + Fourier features for sharp energy thresholds.
  • expmix physics loss — exact / integrated constraints so the network respects stiff Bateman dynamics and mass.
  • Distillation from a stiff Radau ODE teacher on trajectory data.
  • Spectrum-aware σ options (JENDL-5 / EXFOR folding); Joyo σ scaling treated as sensitivity, not primary proof.
  • Split conformal prediction for ~90% relative coverage on Ac-225.

What this is / isn’t

Is: a 0D (lumped) surrogate vs a stiff ODE teacher — useful for fast screening.

Isn’t yet: validated against reactor assay data, a full 3D transport model, or a clinical dosing tool.

Still weak: Ac-227 / thermal impurity channel vs the older v2 model on some anchors.
