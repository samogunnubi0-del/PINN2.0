Physics-Informed Surrogate Modeling for Ra-226 to Ac-225 Transmutation

A computational study of whether a recurrent neural model can reproduce a reduced five-nuclide transmutation system more accurately than a matched non-recurrent control. The long-term goal is faster, auditable screening for Ac-225 production research.

Current evidence-based conclusion

On a frozen synthetic benchmark, the seed-42 V3 LSTM achieved a 0.97% worst-mesh median Ac-225 endpoint error and outperformed a parameter-matched MLP on the predeclared paired median criterion. A hidden test using intentionally different conditions reached a 1.56% median point estimate, but its uncertainty interval did not establish a universal below-3% guarantee.

Frozen tests and audit
  • Physics model: a reduced Ra-226 / Ra-227 / Ac-227 / Ra-225 / Ac-225 chain with spectrum-aware reaction inputs and Bateman dynamics.
  • V3 architecture: an LSTM sequence surrogate constrained by initial conditions, endpoint objectives, atom-budget behavior, and physics residuals.
  • Controlled comparison: 818,442 LSTM parameters versus 797,706 MLP parameters, a 2.53% budget difference, with verified paired scenarios and provenance.
  • Engineering audit: 16 documented defects were repaired or isolated, including Jacobian, shielding, loss scaling, timing-input, random-stream, reporting, and Colab-resume failures.
  • Checkpoint replay: local evaluation reproduced the cloud results; exact Bateman propagation and FP64 Radau agreed near numerical precision for this reduced system.

Scientific Claim Boundary

Supported by the completed experiment

For seed 42 on this reduced synthetic benchmark, the recurrent V3 model met the 1-3% median point-estimate target on both untouched evaluations and beat the matched pointwise control on the declared paired median criterion.

Not yet supported

Universal 1-3% prediction accuracy, real reactor or isotope-production accuracy, general LSTM superiority across seeds, clinical validity, or superiority to exact Bateman, ORIGEN, or OpenMC calculations.

Open reliability problem: matched p95 was 99.6%, locked p95 was 31.2%, and fast-spectrum cases dominated locked failures. Rare exact-zero matched cases also produced false positives as high as 8.09 × 1016 atoms. These failures remain visible in the report.

Where External Collaboration Would Help Most

Highest-value contribution: an independent ORIGEN, OpenMC/depletion, measured irradiation, or production dataset that can test transfer beyond the project's own synthetic generator.

Additional help is welcome with reaction-channel review, uncertainty propagation, fast-spectrum failure analysis, and a confirmatory multi-seed design. The present reduced exact solver is already fast; the next scientific gain is stronger realism and validation, not another claim based only on a lower training loss.
